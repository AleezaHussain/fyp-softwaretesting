\chapter{Results: Comparative Evaluation of Data Center Cooling Techniques}
% \label{ch:results}
%% ============================================================

\noindent This chapter presents the complete simulation results for three data center cooling
techniques: Air-Side Economization, Evaporative Cooling, and Chilled Water System,
evaluated over a full annual horizon of 8{,}760 hours. Results are drawn directly from
the simulation outputs generated at each run, including hourly performance records,
annual summaries, and post-simulation assessment metrics. Each technique is analysed in
detail before a cross-technique comparison and final recommendation are presented.

\section{Air-Side Economization Results}
\label{sec:air}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Technique Overview}

Air-side economization draws outdoor air directly into the data center when ambient
temperature is below the supply air setpoint, eliminating compressor energy during those
hours. When outdoor temperature exceeds the economizer threshold, a mechanical DX unit
supplements or takes over entirely. The simulation used an AI Inference workload profile
generated by CloudSim Plus across 22 server racks with a 5-year financial projection.

\subsection{Energy and PUE Analysis}

The air-side economizer achieved a PUE of 1.291, meaning cooling overhead accounts for
approximately 22.5\% of total facility energy. This is higher than expected for a pure
economizer because the AI Inference workload generates rack-level heat densities of
36--40 kW/rack that exceed the airflow capacity of the economizer system. The cooling
energy breakdown is entirely fan-driven (2{,}097.1 kWh fan energy, 0 kWh mechanical DX
energy in the base case), confirming that when the economizer operates, it does so without
compressor energy. The PUE of 1.291 places this system in the ``Good'' category by industry
standards, though it is constrained by the AI workload density rather than the economizer
design itself.

\subsection{Operating Mode Distribution}

The hourly simulation reveals two distinct operating modes driven by ambient temperature:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{FULL\_ECON} (hours 0--8, 20--23 in the sample day): Outdoor temperature
        $\leq$22\degC. Fan power only, no mechanical cooling. PUE $\approx$ 1.150--1.235.
        This represents the economizer operating at its design intent.
  \item \textbf{MECHANICAL\_ONLY} (hours 9--19): Outdoor temperature $>$25\degC. Full
        mechanical DX cooling engaged. PUE rises to 1.464, a 27\% increase over the
        full-economization mode.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Air-Side Economization: Summary}

Air-side economization delivers zero water consumption and the lowest CAPEX (\$25{,}000)
of the three techniques. However, it is fundamentally constrained by AI workload heat
density. All 22 racks exceeded airflow limits by 60$\times$, the payback period is
economically unviable, and the system requires mechanical backup for 11 hours per day.
It is best suited to low-density enterprise workloads ($<$10 kW/rack) in cool climates,
not high-density AI inference deployments.

\section{Evaporative Cooling Results}
\label{sec:evap}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Technique Overview}

Direct Evaporative Cooling (DEC) lowers supply air temperature by passing warm air through
a water-saturated medium. As water evaporates it absorbs latent heat from the air, reducing
its dry-bulb temperature. The system requires only fan power, no compressor, no
refrigerant. The simulation ran for 8{,}760 hours with a constant ambient temperature of
25\degC and 50\% relative humidity, representing a warm-arid climate baseline, across
50 servers.

\subsection{Energy and PUE Analysis}

The evaporative cooling system achieved a PUE of 1.009, exceptionally close to the
theoretical minimum of 1.000. This result is explained by the operating mode: the system
ran entirely in DEC mode throughout the simulation, meaning the compressor-based DX unit
was never activated. Fan power ranged from approximately 0.135 kW at low IT loads (28 kW,
hours 0--4) to 0.878 kW at peak IT loads (52.3 kW, hour 35), representing a fan overhead
of only 0.48--1.68\% of IT load. This near-linear fan-to-IT relationship is characteristic
of well-designed evaporative systems where fan speed tracks cooling demand.

A PUE of 1.009 is world-class, comparable to the most efficient hyperscale facilities
operated by Google and Meta. The entire cooling overhead of 3{,}452 kWh over a full year
is less than the energy consumed by a single server in one month at peak load.

\subsection{Evaporative Cooling: Summary}

Evaporative cooling delivers the best PUE (1.009), lowest CUE (0.377), zero water
consumption in DEC mode, and 100\% availability. However, it \textbf{fails ASHRAE thermal
compliance for 100\% of the simulation year}, with server inlet temperatures 7--8\degC
above the safe limit. This is a deployment-blocking constraint for standard IT equipment.
The technique is viable only in climates where ambient wet-bulb temperature is sufficiently
low to achieve sub-27\degC inlet temperatures, or when combined with a chilled water
backup for peak conditions.

\section{Chilled Water System Results}
\label{sec:chilled}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Technique Overview}

The chilled water system uses a mechanical refrigeration cycle (chiller) to produce cold
water, which is distributed to CRAHs throughout the data center. A cooling tower rejects
heat to the atmosphere. The system provides precise temperature control regardless of
ambient conditions, making it the industry standard for mission-critical deployments.
The simulation used real hourly weather data spanning a full year, with IT load varying
from approximately 11 kW to 18 kW across the 8{,}760-hour period.

\subsection{Final Recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\textbf{\arabic*.}, itemsep=6pt]
  \item \textbf{Numerical best technique: Evaporative Cooling.}\\
        Achieves PUE 1.009, CUE 0.377, zero water consumption, and lowest carbon intensity.
        Recommended for climates where ambient wet-bulb temperature is consistently low
        enough to maintain server inlet temperatures below 27\degC.

  \item \textbf{Deployment-safe best technique: Chilled Water System.}\\
        The only technique that passes ASHRAE thermal compliance. Recommended for all
        high-density AI/HPC deployments where uptime and hardware reliability are
        non-negotiable. CAPEX and water consumption must be managed through lifecycle
        planning and water recycling.

  \item \textbf{Hybrid recommendation for AI data centers:}\\
        Deploy chilled water as the primary cooling system with evaporative pre-cooling
        during cool/dry periods to reduce chiller load and improve PUE. This hybrid
        approach can achieve PUE 1.05--1.10 while maintaining full thermal compliance,
        combining the efficiency of evaporative cooling with the reliability of chilled water.

  \item \textbf{Air-side economization:}\\
        Not recommended as a standalone solution for AI workloads at the simulated rack
        densities (36--40 kW/rack). Airflow violations on all 22 racks confirm that
        air-side cooling cannot dissipate AI heat densities without liquid cooling
        supplementation. Viable only for low-density enterprise workloads ($<$10 kW/rack)
        in cool climates.
\end{enumerate}

AI & Artificial Intelligence. Compute workloads (inference and training) that drive
  high rack power densities, typically 30--50 kW/rack for GPU-based systems. \\[4pt]

ASHRAE & American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers.
  Publishes thermal guidelines for data center equipment. Class A1 permits server
  inlet temperatures of 15--27\degC. \\[4pt]

CAPEX & Capital Expenditure. One-time upfront investment cost for infrastructure or
  equipment (e.g., chiller plant, cooling tower, piping). \\[4pt]

CFM & Cubic Feet per Minute. Unit of volumetric airflow rate used to quantify the
  air volume delivered to server racks. \\[4pt]

CloudSim Plus & An open-source Java framework for modelling and simulating cloud
  computing infrastructure and workloads, used here to generate realistic AI
  Inference IT load profiles. \\[4pt]

COP & Coefficient of Performance. Ratio of useful cooling output (kW) to electrical
  energy input (kW) for a refrigeration system. Higher is better; typical chillers
  achieve COP 3--8. \\[4pt]

CRAH & Computer Room Air Handler. A precision air-conditioning unit that circulates
  chilled water through a coil to cool data center supply air. \\[4pt]

CUE & Carbon Usage Effectiveness. Kilograms of CO$_2$ emitted per kWh of IT energy
  consumed. Lower is better; measures the carbon intensity of IT work. \\[4pt]

DEC & Direct Evaporative Cooling. A cooling method that lowers air temperature by
  evaporating water into the airstream, requiring only fan power with no compressor
  or refrigerant. \\[4pt]

DX & Direct Expansion. A mechanical refrigeration system where refrigerant expands
  directly in the cooling coil to absorb heat from the air. \\[4pt]

HPC & High-Performance Computing. Compute workloads requiring sustained high CPU/GPU
  utilisation, generating high heat densities per rack. \\[4pt]

LCCP & Life-Cycle Cost of Cooling Plant. Total cost of ownership over the system
  lifetime, including CAPEX, OpEx, and end-of-life costs. \\[4pt]

ML & Machine Learning. A subset of AI in which models learn patterns from data; used
  here for the cooling technique recommender system that assigns recommendation
  probabilities. \\[4pt]

NPV & Net Present Value. The present value of future cash flows minus initial
  investment. A negative NPV indicates the investment does not recover its cost
  at the chosen discount rate over the evaluation period. \\[4pt]

OpEx & Operational Expenditure. Recurring annual costs including electricity, water,
  and maintenance. \\[4pt]

PUE & Power Usage Effectiveness. Ratio of total facility power to IT equipment power.
  PUE = 1.000 is the theoretical ideal (all power goes to IT); lower is better.
  Industry average in 2024 is approximately 1.58. \\[4pt]

WUE & Water Usage Effectiveness. Litres of water consumed per kWh of IT energy
  delivered. Lower is better; measures the water intensity of data center operations. \\
